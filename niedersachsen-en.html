Learn more about Lower Saxony

Lower Saxony is located in northwestern Germany and is the country's second-largest federal state by area. The state stretches from the North Sea coast and the East Frisian Islands to the Harz Mountains in the south. Lower Saxony is particularly known for its North Sea coast, the Wadden Sea, the Lüneburg Heath, the Harz Mountains and its extensive agricultural areas.

Further Information about Lower Saxony

Geography and Landscape

Lower Saxony is located in northwestern Germany and has a highly diverse landscape. To the north, the state borders the North Sea. The coast includes the East Frisian Islands such as Borkum, Norderney, Juist and Langeoog. One of the most important natural regions is the Wadden Sea. It is a coastal landscape that is partly exposed at low tide and covered again by the sea at high tide. The Wadden Sea along the Lower Saxony coast is part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site. Extensive marshlands and fertile areas are found in the northwest. Further inland lies the Lüneburg Heath, known for its characteristic heathlands, forests and small villages. The Harz Mountains are located in southern Lower Saxony. The landscape there is considerably more mountainous than in the rest of the state. Wurmberg, at 971 meters, is the highest elevation in Lower Saxony. Important rivers such as the Elbe, Weser, Ems, Aller and Leine flow through Lower Saxony. These rivers shape many regions of the state and are also important for agriculture, navigation and nature.

History of Lower Saxony

The history of Lower Saxony is closely connected with historic Saxony and various principalities in northern Germany. The former Kingdom of Hanover played a particularly important role. Its capital, Hanover, is now the capital of Lower Saxony. The cities of Braunschweig, Lüneburg and Osnabrück also played important historical roles. Lüneburg became wealthy during the Middle Ages through the salt trade. Osnabrück is known as one of the places where the Peace of Westphalia was negotiated in 1648, bringing an end to the Thirty Years' War. After the Second World War, Lower Saxony was established as a new federal state in 1946. It was formed from several historic territories, including Hanover, Oldenburg, Braunschweig and Schaumburg-Lippe. The history of the state can still be seen today in numerous old towns, castles, palaces, churches and historic buildings.

Population and Cities

Lower Saxony has around eight million inhabitants and includes both major cities and extensive rural areas. The population is particularly concentrated in larger cities and in the areas around Hanover, Braunschweig and Osnabrück. Hanover is the capital and largest city of the state. It is an important center for trade fairs, companies, universities and services. The Hanover Fair is especially well known and is one of Germany's major industrial trade fairs. Braunschweig is an important center in southeastern Lower Saxony and has a long history as a former residence city. Wolfsburg is particularly known for its automotive industry and Volkswagen. Other important cities include Göttingen, Oldenburg, Osnabrück, Hildesheim, Salzgitter and Wilhelmshaven. Wilhelmshaven has an important deep-water port on the North Sea and therefore plays a significant role in maritime trade.

Economy

The economy of Lower Saxony is strongly connected with the automotive industry, agriculture, food production, logistics and wind energy. The automotive industry plays a particularly important role. Wolfsburg is Volkswagen's main production location and is one of Germany's best-known automotive centers. Numerous companies in other parts of Lower Saxony also manufacture vehicle components and technical products. Lower Saxony is also one of Germany's major agricultural regions. Particularly in the northwest, many farms produce milk, meat, grain, potatoes and other agricultural products. Agriculture is closely connected with the food-processing industry.

Its long North Sea coastline also gives Lower Saxony favorable conditions for wind energy. Several offshore wind farms are located off the coast, consisting of wind turbines installed in the sea. Ports are also important to the economy. Wilhelmshaven is home to JadeWeserPort, one of Germany's most important deep-water ports. The port can accommodate very large container ships and connects Lower Saxony with international trade routes. Hanover is also an important center for trade fairs, services and commerce. The combination of different economic sectors makes Lower Saxony a highly diverse federal state economically.

Nature and Environment

Lower Saxony contains some of the most important natural landscapes in northern Germany. Along the North Sea coast lies Lower Saxony Wadden Sea National Park. It protects extensive areas of mudflats, salt marshes, dunes and coastal waters. The area provides an important habitat for numerous bird species and other wildlife. The East Frisian Islands are also among the particularly protected coastal landscapes. Their beaches, dunes and salt marshes are sensitive habitats protected within the national park. A very different landscape can be found in the Lüneburg Heath. Its extensive heathlands developed through traditional land use and are maintained today through landscape management. During the blooming season in late summer, the heath changes its appearance dramatically. In the south, the Harz Mountains contain forests, mountains, streams and valleys. Harz National Park protects large areas of natural woodland there. The different landscapes stretching from the North Sea to the Harz make Lower Saxony a particularly diverse natural region.

Culture and Attractions

Lower Saxony has a diverse cultural heritage shaped by its northern German history, former ruling dynasties, Hanseatic cities and proximity to the North Sea. In Hanover, the Herrenhausen Gardens are among the best-known attractions. The Baroque gardens form a large historic garden complex and are one of the most important cultural destinations in the state capital. Another well-known landmark is Marienburg Castle near Hanover. The neo-Gothic castle was built in the 19th century for the royal family of Hanover and stands on a wooded hill, giving it a distinctive setting. The city of Goslar in the Harz has a historic old town with numerous well-preserved half-timbered houses. Together with the Rammelsberg Mines and the Upper Harz Water Management System, it forms a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Lüneburg's old town is also known for its historic brick and half-timbered buildings. Much of the city's historical importance came from the salt trade, which made Lüneburg an important medieval trading center. Along the coast, the East Frisian Islands, especially Norderney and Borkum, are well-known tourist destinations. The Wadden Sea also offers visitors unique opportunities to experience the coastal landscape and its wildlife. In southern Lower Saxony, the Harz Mountains and the city of Goslar are important destinations for nature and cultural tourism. The combination of the North Sea coast, heathlands, historic cities and the Harz gives Lower Saxony a distinctive and varied cultural profile.
