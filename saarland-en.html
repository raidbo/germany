Learn more about Saarland

Saarland is located in southwestern Germany and borders France and Luxembourg. This small federal state is particularly characterized by its proximity to neighboring countries, its industrial history, its forests and its varied landscapes. Among its best-known attractions are the Saar Loop, the UNESCO World Heritage Site Völklingen Ironworks and its French-influenced cultural traditions.

Geography and Landscape

Saarland is located in southwestern Germany and borders Rhineland-Palatinate to the north and east. To the west and south, it shares borders with Luxembourg and France. This location gives the federal state close connections with its European neighboring regions. The landscape is predominantly hilly and is characterized by forests, river valleys and agricultural areas. The Saar flows through the federal state and is the most important river in the region. One of the best-known landscapes is the Saar Loop near Mettlach, where the Saar forms a striking bend through the wooded landscape. In the north lies the natural region of the Schwarzwälder Hochwald. The highest mountain in Saarland is Dollberg, with an elevation of around 695 meters.

History of Saarland

The history of Saarland was strongly shaped by its location between Germany and France as well as by its rich deposits of coal and iron ore. Particularly during the 19th and 20th centuries, the region developed into an important center of the coal and steel industry. After the First World War, the Saar Territory was placed under international administration. In 1935, following a referendum, it returned to Germany. After the Second World War, Saarland initially received a special political status and became economically closely connected with France. Following another referendum and political negotiations, Saarland became politically part of the Federal Republic of Germany on January 1, 1957. Its economic integration into the Federal Republic followed in 1959.

Population and Cities

Saarland is the smallest German federal state by area and is also one of the smaller states in terms of population. The population is particularly concentrated in the densely populated area along the Saar, which connects several cities and municipalities. Saarbrücken is the state capital and the most important political, economic and cultural center of Saarland. Other significant cities include Saarlouis, Neunkirchen, Homburg, Völklingen, St. Ingbert and Merzig. Völklingen is particularly known for its former ironworks, which is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Economy

The economy of Saarland was shaped for a long time by coal mining and the steel industry. Although hard-coal mining has now ended, industry and manufacturing remain important parts of the economy. Today, the automotive industry, steel production, mechanical engineering, metal processing and the energy sector play particularly important roles. The service sector is also becoming increasingly significant. Due to its location on the borders with France and Luxembourg, Saarland also has particular importance for cross-border economic relations. Saarbrücken is an important center for trade, services and international cooperation.

Nature and Environment

Despite its small size, Saarland has diverse natural landscapes with extensive forests, river valleys and protected areas. Particularly characteristic are the large forested areas in the north and the landscapes along the Saar and its tributaries. An important protected area is the Saar-Hunsrück Nature Park, which extends across large parts of northern Saarland. The surroundings of the Saar Loop near Mettlach are another well-known natural area, characterized by wooded slopes and the deeply incised Saar Valley. The protection of forests, waterways and species-rich landscapes plays an important role in Saarland. At the same time, former industrial sites are increasingly being opened up for nature, recreation and new uses.

Culture and Attractions

Saarland has a distinctive cultural character shaped by German and French influences as well as by the industrial history of the region. This is particularly visible in its cities, historic buildings and former industrial sites. One of the best-known attractions is the Völklingen Ironworks, a former ironworks and now a UNESCO World Heritage Site. It is one of Europe's most important industrial monuments and provides insight into the history of iron and steel production. Another landmark of Saarland is the Saar Loop near Mettlach, one of the region's best-known landscape and viewing destinations. In Saarbrücken, Saarbrücken Castle, the Ludwigskirche and the historic old town are important cultural attractions. Roman and medieval remains in various towns, as well as regional festivals and culinary traditions, are also part of Saarland's cultural heritage.
